# Choose overlap hours or follow-the-sun coverage for offshore support

Hiring in the Philippines creates scheduling options, but a time-zone difference does not automatically create round-the-clock productivity. Follow-the-sun coverage works when one team can leave a complete, actionable packet for the next. Live overlap works when the work depends on rapid questions, coaching, or approvals. Most teams need a deliberate blend rather than a slogan.

The choice should start with the work and its decision path. Map when requests arrive, when customers expect responses, who can approve exceptions, and which tasks can continue without a live manager. Then design hours around those facts and applicable employment arrangements.

## Measure decision latency before choosing a schedule

Review a representative week of work. For each item, record arrival time, first useful action, questions raised, decision owner, response time, completion, and any customer deadline. Separate active handling from waiting. A queue may appear slow because an offshore specialist takes eight hours to finish, when seven of those hours were spent waiting for an onshore approval.

Group questions by type. Missing source information suggests an intake problem. Repeated policy questions suggest incomplete instructions. True exceptions need a decision owner. Only the last category necessarily benefits from live access to a manager, and even then the business should define how quickly the decision is required.

## When overlap is the better fit

Choose meaningful overlap when the role is new, instructions are still changing, cases are ambiguous, or the work involves frequent live collaboration. Candidate interviews, incident coordination, complex customer cases, and the first weeks of a new process often need shared time.

Overlap should have a purpose. Reserve a window for queue review, coaching, decisions, and handoff. Do not require a Philippines-based employee to mirror an overseas day simply so both calendars appear green. If two hours resolve the day's exceptions, define those two hours and leave the remaining schedule for focused work.

Write the window in both local time zones and identify which zone controls the agreement. Account for daylight-saving changes in the overseas location. Confirm working hours, breaks, holidays, overtime, and schedule changes through the proper employment and people processes.

## When sequential coverage can work

Follow-the-sun is strongest when inputs are stable, work is visible in a shared system, authority is bounded, and acceptance criteria are clear. An onshore team can leave a reviewed queue; the Philippines team processes permitted items and prepares exceptions; the next onshore shift makes reserved decisions.

The handoff must contain more than a status. Include the source, current state, work completed, evidence, decision needed, consequence of delay, and safe next action. Assign an owner for every open question. “Please check” is not an operational handoff.

Create a cutoff for new work. Items received after it either enter the next cycle or follow a documented urgent route. Without a cutoff, the outgoing team keeps adding work while the incoming team tries to establish priorities.

## Compare two support cases

Consider a customer support queue. Password-reset requests with verified identity and an approved procedure may move sequentially. A billing complaint that requires a discretionary credit waits for the authorized owner. If that owner never overlaps with the support shift, the customer promise must reflect the next decision window rather than implying immediate resolution.

Now consider a recruiting coordinator. Resume administration and interview scheduling can continue from approved records. A change to selection criteria needs the hiring owner. Live overlap may be useful for interview-day changes, while ordinary confirmations can follow the queue. One role can therefore use both models by task.

## Design the handoff contract

Define a small set of states: ready, active, waiting for evidence, waiting for decision, ready for review, and closed. Each state needs a named owner and next-review time. Use timestamps with zone labels. Link to the authoritative record instead of copying customer or candidate data into informal messages.

Require the outgoing person to identify the top priorities and any deadline that occurs before the next overlap. Require the incoming person to acknowledge receipt and flag unusable items. Track rejected handoffs by reason so the team can improve the source, not just remind people to “communicate better.”

Set an urgent path with a narrow definition. Name the channel, decision owner, backup, expected response, and what the specialist may do while waiting. Urgent should describe consequence and timing, not the requester's seniority.

## Pilot with service measures that expose waiting

Test the schedule on a bounded queue. Measure time to first useful action, handling time, time waiting by owner, first-review acceptance, reopen rate, handoff rejection, and missed customer commitments. Report denominators and the measurement period. A higher item count is not an improvement if rework or customer waiting rises.

Ask the staff member and manager where live time was genuinely required. Move routine questions into instructions, examples, or decision tables. Preserve overlap for work that benefits from conversation. If the manager becomes the overnight bottleneck, add a qualified backup or narrow the promised service window rather than pretending coverage exists.

## Review human sustainability and resilience

A schedule must be operationally useful and sustainable. Review transport, home-working conditions, planned leave, local holidays, health and safety processes, and backup coverage through appropriate company policies. Do not base the design on an assumption that a remote employee is always available.

Test an absence on each side. Can the offshore team continue safe work when the onshore reviewer is away? Can the onshore team understand the queue when the Philippines specialist is unavailable? A schedule with no documented backup is a single point of failure regardless of its nominal coverage hours.

The final choice should state which tasks require overlap, which move sequentially, the promised response windows, the handoff cutoff, and the owners of exceptions. Revisit it when volume, customer promises, systems, or authority changes.
