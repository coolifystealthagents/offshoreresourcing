# Design backup coverage for a one-person offshore function

A capable offshore specialist can become a hidden single point of failure. The work runs smoothly while that person is available, so managers postpone documenting queues, granting backup access, and deciding what can wait. Planned leave or an unexpected outage then reveals that the “process” lived in one person's calendar and memory.

Backup coverage does not require duplicating a full-time role. It requires a minimum service, a continuation record, a prepared backup, and a tested way to restore normal operations.

## Define the minimum service first

List the outcomes the function produces and rank them by consequence of delay. Separate work that must continue the same day, work that can pause for a stated period, and work that can be rescheduled. Include deadlines, customer promises, regulatory or contractual dependencies, and upstream teams waiting for the output.

Be specific. “Cover the inbox” is not a service. “Acknowledge complete requests within the published window, route urgent exceptions to the named manager, and preserve all other requests for return” is. During an absence, the backup may maintain safety and continuity without matching normal throughput.

Agree who can declare the reduced-service mode and who communicates it. Do not let each requester negotiate a different priority with the backup.

## Inventory the continuity package

The primary specialist should maintain a current queue with item, source, status, owner, next action, deadline, exception, and evidence. Recurring work needs a calendar, trigger, procedure, expected output, reviewer, and failure route. Decision rules must identify what the backup may execute and what remains reserved.

Link to authoritative records instead of creating a shadow archive. A backup should not need the primary person's personal notes, browser bookmarks, or local downloads. Store approved templates, examples, and contact routes where both can reach them.

Add a “return briefing” field for work that may pause. This prevents the backup from spending limited time reconstructing low-priority context and gives the primary person a clean restart.

## Choose a backup by capability and access

Select someone who can perform the minimum service within their role, schedule, and authority. The backup may be another Philippines-based team member, an onshore colleague, or a shared service owner. Confirm workload capacity rather than adding coverage as an invisible extra duty.

Map required systems and data. Provision individual least-privilege access before the absence, or maintain an approved just-in-time process that has been tested. Never share the primary person's password. Confirm multifactor authentication, device readiness, support contacts, and access expiry or recertification.

Some decisions may have a different backup from the operational work. The person triaging a queue does not automatically receive authority to approve payments, commitments, or policy exceptions. Record both roles.

## Prepare three absence scenarios

For planned leave, use a cutoff, reconcile the queue, assign work, review upcoming deadlines, and conduct a short handoff. The primary person can answer questions before leaving, but the package should still stand on its own.

For an unexpected one-day absence, the manager activates the minimum service from the shared record. The backup handles urgent and time-bound work, marks anything paused, and records decisions needed. Avoid contacting the absent employee unless the company's policy and the genuine circumstance make that appropriate.

For an extended absence, review capacity, customer communication, access, and work redistribution. A one-day workaround may be unsafe for two weeks. Name the person authorized to change the coverage plan and the threshold that triggers that review.

## Run a tabletop before leave occurs

Give the backup a fictional or controlled live case. Ask them to find the queue, locate the source, perform one permitted action, identify an exception, contact the decision owner, and leave a continuation note. Confirm that one prohibited action remains unavailable.

Observe where the backup hesitates. Missing terminology, stale links, unclear priorities, and inaccessible templates are process defects. Repair them and repeat the affected step. A checklist marked complete is weaker evidence than a backup who can actually continue an item.

Include a communication failure or unavailable manager. The backup should know the secondary route and the safe action while waiting. If every exception depends on one manager, the function still has a single point of failure.

## Measure the coverage event

Record items received, items completed, items paused by category, missed commitments, decision waiting, rework after return, and any access problem. Compare results with the declared minimum service, not full normal output. Ask requesters whether routing was clear.

On return, the primary specialist reviews exceptions and resumed work rather than silently correcting everything. Update the continuity package from observed gaps. Remove temporary access when appropriate and preserve only the evidence required by policy.

Use patterns across events. If urgent volume consistently exceeds backup capacity, the minimum service is unrealistic. If most work can pause safely, the organization may not need expensive duplicate coverage. If handoffs repeatedly fail because sources are missing, repair intake before adding people.

## Protect sustainable work

Backup design should support leave, not make employees feel permanently on call. Publish coverage to stakeholders, set response expectations, and respect approved working hours and local holidays. A Philippines-based team member should not have to monitor work during leave because the business chose not to prepare.

Review the plan when systems, volume, authority, staff, or customer commitments change. Schedule a lightweight retest and recertify access. The plan is ready when a manager can activate it without private knowledge and the backup can deliver the agreed minimum safely.
