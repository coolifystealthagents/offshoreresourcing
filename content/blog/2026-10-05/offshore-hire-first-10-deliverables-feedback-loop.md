# Set a feedback loop for an offshore hire's first 10 deliverables

The first ten real outputs teach a new hire what the company actually values. If reviews arrive late or consist of silent edits, the employee learns to wait for rescue. If every small preference is treated as an error, the person learns to optimize for one reviewer's taste. A deliberate feedback loop turns early work into evidence about the employee, the instructions, and the review system.

Ten is a practical observation window, not a universal threshold. Use enough varied work to include ordinary cases and exceptions, then make an evidence-based release decision. Keep higher-risk actions under their required controls regardless of the count.

## Define a deliverable before counting it

Name the output, source, permitted action, acceptance criteria, reviewer, and evidence. A deliverable should be a complete unit that a reviewer can accept or return. Reading a guide or attending a call is onboarding activity, not an operational output.

Choose a sequence that represents the role. Begin with lower-consequence cases, then introduce realistic variation. For a recruiting coordinator, the set might include interview packs, schedule confirmations, a conflicting-calendar case, and an incomplete candidate record. Do not count ten copies of the easiest case and infer readiness for the full queue.

## Establish the review promise

Tell the employee who reviews each item, how quickly feedback should arrive, where it will be recorded, and what may continue while waiting. Give the reviewer backup coverage. Early learning stalls when the employee submits at the end of a Philippines shift and the onshore manager does not respond until days later.

Review against the criteria shared in advance. Mark the result accepted, accepted with a non-blocking note, or returned for a named correction. Avoid “looks good” and “needs work.” The first hides why the output succeeded; the second gives no usable next action.

## Use reasons that identify the repair

Create a short return vocabulary such as missing source, incorrect source, omitted field, unsupported assumption, missed exception, action outside authority, unclear communication, or incomplete evidence. Add a specific note and link to the affected part.

The reason should point toward the owner of improvement. Missing source data may require an intake fix. Conflicting examples require an instruction owner. A repeated skipped step after clear training may require individual coaching. Do not label every failure as carelessness when the workflow itself is unreliable.

Preserve the original submission and the corrected version. Silent reviewer edits destroy the learning trail. The employee should make the correction where safe, explain the change, and resubmit for confirmation.

## Vary the sequence intentionally

Deliverables one and two can test the basic path with guided review. Three and four should establish whether feedback carries forward. Five can introduce a missing input. Six can test prioritization. Seven can include a genuine exception. Eight can require a handoff. Nine can be completed with the normal review cadence, and ten can test independent work within current authority.

That sequence is only an example. Match it to the actual lane. A finance-support role may require more supervised samples because the consequence is higher. A simple administrative queue may reach bounded independence sooner. Document why the sample is sufficient.

## Run a short review conversation

For each return, discuss the intended outcome, observed evidence, impact, required correction, and next demonstration. Let the employee restate the rule in their own words. If they cannot, improve the explanation before adding another case.

Keep feedback close to the work. Batch comments can reveal patterns, but waiting until all ten are finished allows the same misunderstanding to repeat. Separate urgent correction from a broader coaching theme so the employee knows what must change now.

Ask what made the task hard: unavailable access, ambiguous source, unclear priority, unfamiliar terminology, or a genuine skill gap. The answer does not erase the result, but it changes the appropriate repair.

## Measure learning, not reviewer preference

Track first-review acceptance, returns by reason, correction acceptance, time in employee control, time waiting for review, correctly identified exceptions, and repeated reason codes. Use denominators. “Two errors” means something different across three outputs and thirty.

Look for transfer. After feedback on a missing source reference, does the next relevant deliverable include it without prompting? Improvement across comparable cases is stronger evidence than one polished item produced under step-by-step direction.

Audit reviewer consistency too. If one reviewer returns formatting that another accepts, settle the standard. If a preference does not affect the outcome, decide whether it belongs in acceptance criteria at all.

## Make a bounded release decision

After the observation window, review the complete evidence. Possible decisions include release to normal sampling, continue full review for a defined reason, narrow the task lane, repair instructions and repeat selected cases, or escalate a performance concern through the proper people process.

Do not convert accurate preparation into new approval authority automatically. The person may be ready to complete standard records while financial, contractual, personnel, or policy decisions remain with named owners.

State the next quality-control level: for example, review every high-risk case and a random sample of standard cases. Define what triggers a return to closer review, such as a system change, new task type, missed escalation, or extended absence.

## Keep records proportionate

Store operational evidence in approved systems and personnel-sensitive notes in their authorized location. Limit access and retention. Do not place credentials, private customer data, or unnecessary personal commentary in a coaching tracker. The Philippine National Privacy Commission's resources can inform privacy review for personal data handled in the Philippines.

The loop succeeds when the employee can explain the standard, apply feedback to a later case, recognize an exception, and produce evidence another reviewer can follow. It also succeeds when the company discovers and fixes a broken instruction before blaming the hire.
