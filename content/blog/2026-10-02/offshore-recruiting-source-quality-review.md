# Review candidate source quality in an offshore recruiting lane

A list of forty profiles can be less useful than five candidates with clear evidence. Volume says that a search happened. It does not show whether the sourcer understood the role, used a reproducible method, controlled duplicates, or handed the manager enough context to choose the next step.

Review source quality through the search record and manager outcomes. Keep hiring authority with the buyer. The offshore sourcer prepares evidence, maintains records, and improves the search from observed feedback.

## Freeze the role evidence before searching

Turn the intake into requirements a reviewer can recognize. Separate must-have evidence, useful experience, trainable gaps, location or schedule constraints, compensation boundaries supplied by the buyer, and questions that need manager judgment. Link each requirement to the approved role brief.

Do not ask a sourcer to infer a requirement from the previous hire. The incumbent may have skills that are helpful but unnecessary. Likewise, a title can hide very different work. Describe the tasks, systems, outputs, and decision boundaries behind the title.

Record unresolved choices. If the manager has not decided whether industry experience is required, the sourcer can run two labelled search lanes. Quietly treating the preference as mandatory may remove useful candidates before the manager sees the tradeoff.

## Preserve the search path

For each search, record the channel, date, query or filters, market limits, and number of profiles reviewed. Save meaningful query revisions. A manager should be able to see how the sourcer moved from the role evidence to the pool.

Search records support diagnosis. If a channel returns many senior profiles for a coordinator role, the team can change filters or try another source. Without the record, low relevance becomes a vague argument about effort.

Do not present channel counts as market facts. Search results depend on query design, platform coverage, profile freshness, and access. Label what the search can and cannot establish.

## Require evidence for inclusion

Every submitted profile should show which requirement it appears to support and where the evidence came from. Use a short note rather than copying an entire profile. Distinguish explicit evidence, a reasonable question for screening, and missing information.

For example, a profile may show calendar coordination across several executives but say nothing about the scheduling platform. The note can mark coordination as supported and the tool as unconfirmed. It should not turn silence into either mastery or failure.

The sourcer should also explain material concerns, such as unclear dates or a schedule conflict, without making a hiring finding. The manager decides whether the evidence merits contact, screening, or exclusion.

## Sample exclusions as carefully as inclusions

Quality review often inspects only submitted candidates. That misses an important failure: suitable people excluded by a misunderstood rule. Take a small sample from each major exclusion reason and compare it with the brief.

Use specific reason codes. "Not qualified" is too broad to teach anyone. Better reasons refer to missing required evidence, incompatible working hours, duplicate record, location constraint supplied by the buyer, compensation mismatch based on approved information, or an unresolved question sent to the manager.

Review borderline exclusions during calibration. If reviewers disagree, fix the requirement or add examples. Do not force false certainty into the candidate record merely to simplify reporting.

## Control duplicates and stale records

Choose the fields and systems used to identify possible duplicates. A different email address does not always mean a different person, and a matching name does not prove a duplicate. Flag the record for review when identity is uncertain.

Preserve prior outreach, consent or communication status where applicable, recruiter ownership, and the reason a record was closed. The sourcer should not restart contact simply because a candidate appeared through another channel.

Set a freshness rule. Old skills, availability, location, and interest may no longer be current. Mark historical evidence as historical and verify what matters through the approved process before the team relies on it.

## Inspect the complete handoff

A useful handoff gives the manager the role, candidate source, evidence summary, open questions, duplicate check, communication state, and requested decision. It does not bury uncertainty beneath a confident recommendation.

Define the next decisions. The manager may choose outreach, request more evidence, hold, redirect to another role, or exclude with a recorded reason. Give each decision a due time so good candidates do not sit in an invisible queue.

When a manager returns a profile, capture the reason against the requirement. Private comments such as "not what I imagined" cannot improve the next search. Ask for the observable mismatch or update the role brief when the requirement changed.

## Run a calibration sample

Select profiles from different channels, inclusion decisions, and exclusion reasons. Have the sourcer and manager assess them independently, then compare evidence. Discuss the largest disagreements first.

One sample might involve a customer support candidate with strong written examples but no named experience in the buyer's ticketing tool. The role brief says comparable queue work is required and the specific tool is trainable. If the sourcer excluded the person for the tool, the correction belongs in the search rule.

Repeat calibration after a role change, a new channel, or a pattern of manager returns. Calibration is not a one-time training event. It keeps the working interpretation tied to the approved role.

## Measure usefulness rather than list size

Track profiles reviewed, submitted, accepted for the next step, returned by reason, duplicates, stale records, and decision waiting. Use rates with denominators and keep channels separate where their coverage differs.

Time-to-list can matter, but speed should not reward weak evidence. Pair it with manager acceptance and later screening outcomes. Avoid claiming that sourcing caused a hire; later interviews and buyer decisions affect the funnel.

Review the effort spent on unclear intake. Repeated search revisions may show that the brief changed, not that the sourcer performed poorly. Record the version used for each search.

## Keep candidate data controlled

Use approved systems, individual accounts, least privilege, controlled exports, and defined retention. Avoid copying candidate information into informal trackers. Route access, deletion, correction, and privacy questions to authorized owners.

The Philippine National Privacy Commission's Data Privacy Act resources can inform qualified review for Philippine handling, while the buyer must also assess other applicable jurisdictions, contracts, and hiring rules. This guide does not decide those obligations.

During provider discovery, ask for a fictional search record and a calibration exercise. Review how the provider handles unclear requirements, duplicate candidates, restricted data, manager delays, and corrections.
