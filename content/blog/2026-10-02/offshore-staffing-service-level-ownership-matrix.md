# Build a Service-Level Ownership Matrix for an Offshore Team

A service-level target is easy to write and surprisingly easy to misunderstand. “Reply within four hours” might describe the first acknowledgement, a useful answer, or a completed resolution. It might apply around the clock or only during an agreed coverage window. An offshore coordinator can appear late even when the real delay is an approval sitting with a manager. Before attaching a number to performance, convert the promise into an ownership matrix that shows who acts, who decides, what evidence closes the clock, and what happens when the normal path breaks.

This exercise is useful before hiring, during provider discovery, and when an existing offshore queue produces arguments about responsiveness. It does not transfer commercial, legal, security, or customer authority to the offshore role. It makes those boundaries visible enough to operate and review.

## Begin with the customer event, not an abstract metric

Choose one event that matters to the person waiting. For an order-support queue, that event may be a customer reporting that a shipment has not arrived. Record the channel, required fields, business hours, and consequence of delay. Then define four separate moments: receipt, acknowledgement, decision-ready preparation, and final resolution. One headline target should not hide all four.

Suppose the customer writes at 3:40 p.m. in the buyer’s timezone. The offshore coordinator confirms the order number, carrier status, promised date, and approved contact details. The coordinator can acknowledge receipt and prepare a case, but a refund above an agreed limit remains with the customer-service manager. The matrix should stop the preparation clock when the evidence package is complete and start a separate decision clock for the manager. Otherwise the coordinator is measured for authority the role never received.

Use real recent cases to identify the event. A policy document alone rarely shows missing attachments, conflicting records, duplicate requests, or messages arriving through the wrong channel. Sample completed, returned, escalated, and abandoned work. The differences reveal which timestamps can be trusted and which service promises currently depend on informal memory.

## Name five kinds of ownership

Queue ownership means keeping the item visible, correctly classified, and assigned. Work ownership means performing the permitted research or administration. Decision ownership means approving an exception, commitment, payment, access change, or customer remedy. Communication ownership means sending an approved message from an approved channel. Evidence ownership means confirming that the record is complete enough to close. One person can hold several roles, but the matrix should not assume that they do.

For each stage, write a primary owner and a backup. Avoid team names such as “operations” when no individual on duty knows they carry the decision. Include the coverage window and handoff trigger. If the primary manager is unavailable after a defined interval, the offshore coordinator needs an approved route to a backup decision owner—not permission to invent a decision.

An effective row can be read as a sentence: when a complete priority-two request arrives during the service window, the coordinator validates four required fields within thirty minutes; the account owner decides any contractual exception within two hours; the coordinator sends the approved outcome; and the quality reviewer samples the closed record weekly. That is more useful than a red, amber, or green cell without operating meaning.

## Define clocks that people can reproduce

Every clock needs a start event, stop event, timezone, service calendar, source system, pause rule, and treatment of reopened work. Decide whether an automated receipt starts the acknowledgement clock. State whether weekends and holidays count. If the requester omits a required field, record a visible waiting-for-requester state rather than silently resetting the timestamp.

Pause rules deserve particular care because they can make weak service look healthy. A pause should have a reason code, evidence, owner, and next follow-up time. “Waiting” is not enough. Distinguish waiting for customer information, internal approval, third-party action, system recovery, and scheduled future work. Review long pauses separately from active handling time.

Reopened cases should retain their history. If a coordinator closes an item too early and the requester returns it, a fresh ticket must not erase the failed outcome. Decide whether the original resolution clock continues, whether a correction clock begins, and which defect category applies. Preserved history supports coaching and process repair without turning every correction into personal blame.

## Separate normal work from exception authority

Write the normal lane first. List the systems the offshore role may use, records it may update, messages it may send, and conditions under which it may close an item. Then add explicit stop points: missing identity evidence, conflicting source records, suspected fraud, sensitive personal data in an unapproved channel, a requested commitment outside policy, or a change that cannot be reversed.

Each stop point needs an immediate safe action. The coordinator might restrict access to the record, preserve the original message, avoid repeating sensitive details, use an approved holding response, and notify the named owner. The matrix should set an escalation expectation without implying that an offshore team member is the privacy, security, legal, or financial decision maker.

Test the route with a difficult case. Imagine the source system says an account is active while the billing record says it is suspended, and the customer asks for urgent access. A good matrix directs the coordinator to preserve both records, avoid changing access, assemble the relevant evidence, and route the conflict to the authorized account and security owners. It also defines who updates the customer and when. Speed comes from prepared authority, not improvisation.

## Measure the handoff as well as the worker

Report active handling, decision waiting, requester waiting, third-party waiting, and correction time separately. Add acceptance rate, return reasons, reopened rate, aged exceptions, and the proportion of work missing required intake fields. A single average can conceal a small set of cases that create most customer risk.

Use medians and a relevant upper percentile when volume allows, but retain the underlying case evidence. Compare like periods and label changes in volume, channel mix, operating hours, or scope. A faster result after easy work moved into the queue does not prove the process improved. Likewise, slower handling after adding a complex service lane does not prove the team deteriorated.

The manager should review their own decision latency beside offshore handling. If complete cases wait overnight for approval, adding another coordinator will not resolve the constraint. The practical response may be a backup approver, a narrower exception threshold, better mobile access for the manager, or a revised promise to the customer.

## Run a two-week calibration before making a promise

Select a bounded set of representative work. Include routine items, incomplete requests, conflicting evidence, an urgent case, and one item that should be refused or redirected. Before launch, have the coordinator and manager classify the same examples independently. Differences expose vague priority definitions and hidden authority assumptions.

During calibration, review a short daily sample. Ask whether the correct clock started, the right owner received the handoff, the evidence was sufficient, the message stayed within approval, and closure was justified. Repair the matrix when the process is unclear; coach the individual when the process is clear but was not followed. Keep those diagnoses separate.

At the end, choose deliberately: adopt the service level, narrow its scope, change coverage, strengthen manager capacity, or continue the pilot. Do not turn the end date into automatic approval. Record the assumptions behind the target so a later change in volume or authority triggers review.

## Questions to take into provider discovery

Ask a provider to demonstrate how it distinguishes acknowledgement from resolution, records pauses, manages manager-owned decisions, preserves reopened history, and covers an absent primary worker. Request a sample matrix and a fictional exception walkthrough rather than relying only on a general claim about responsiveness. Confirm which reporting comes from the source system and which is manually prepared.

Also confirm access controls, individual identities, multifactor authentication, export restrictions, quality sampling, incident escalation, and removal ownership. The Philippine National Privacy Commission’s Data Privacy Act resources, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, and CISA account-security guidance provide useful primary references for a qualified privacy and security review. They do not replace advice for the buyer’s jurisdictions, contracts, sector, or facts.

The finished matrix should let a second authorized reader reconstruct one case from arrival to closure. If it cannot show who held the item, who made the decision, which evidence supported it, and why the clock stopped, the target is not yet operational.
