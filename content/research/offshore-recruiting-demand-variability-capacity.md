## Decision in brief

A buyer should not size offshore recruiting support from the average number of requisitions alone. The useful planning unit is work demand by stage and week: new requisitions, sourcing requests, screening records, scheduling events, candidate follow-ups, and exceptions. Average demand hides the bursts that create stale candidates and rushed decisions. A defensible plan combines a recent arrival history, the effort distribution for each work type, a named service window, and an explicit reserve for exceptions.

The practical conclusion is conditional. A stable, well-defined queue may support a repeatable staffing ratio. A changing role mix, irregular hiring approvals, or concentrated interview days needs more reserve or a narrower promise. This article offers an operating method, not a universal Philippines productivity benchmark or a guarantee about an individual worker.

## Why averages mislead

Suppose a team opens eight requisitions in a month. That figure says little about whether all eight arrive on one Monday, whether they are repeats of known roles, or whether every hiring manager changes the brief after sourcing starts. Workload is created by transactions and uncertainty, not merely by open positions. A coordinator may handle ten clean scheduling events faster than one event involving four time zones, an unavailable interviewer, and a candidate deadline.

Queueing research explains why delay can rise sharply as utilization approaches full capacity. The exact curve depends on the arrival and service distributions, so a buyer should not import a formula without checking assumptions. The management implication is simpler: planning every paid hour as productive handling time leaves no room for uneven arrivals, clarification, quality checks, breaks, training, or system incidents.

## Define the demand record

Create one record for each work request entering the offshore lane. Record the request type, arrival timestamp, required completion window, role family, priority reason, input completeness, first-touch time, active handling time where measurable, waiting time, exception type, outcome, and rework. Avoid surveillance-style minute tracking. The aim is to distinguish work from waiting and expose missing inputs, not to infer effort from online presence.

Use the same entry rule throughout the observation period. If a requisition counts only after approval, do not mix draft requests into the denominator. If candidate follow-ups are bundled, state the bundle rule. Preserve counts as well as rates. A weekly 90th percentile based on a handful of events is unstable and should be labeled accordingly.

## Segment before forecasting

Separate work that behaves differently. Sourcing for a recurring administrative profile is not interchangeable with sourcing for a scarce technical specialty. Interview scheduling with one interviewer differs from a panel across regions. Clean requests differ from requests missing compensation, working hours, must-have criteria, or an accountable decision owner.

A useful first segmentation is standard, complex, and exception work. Define those labels with observable fields rather than judgment after the fact. For example, a standard scheduling request might have one approved duration, named participants, stated time zones, and three candidate windows. An exception might lack an interviewer, conflict with a candidate deadline, or require a change after confirmation.

## Estimate workable capacity

Start with scheduled paid time, then subtract recurring commitments that cannot process the queue: team meetings, required training, approved breaks, system administration, and quality review. Do not assume the remainder is continuously usable. Apply an occupancy target that leaves room for variation and record it as a planning assumption, not a fact.

Convert demand into workload using observed handling-time ranges by segment. Medians describe typical work but can understate the long tail, so examine upper percentiles and the causes of slow cases. When reliable handling time is unavailable, use completed units per comparable week and make a conservative pilot estimate. Review the estimate after the process stabilizes.

The capacity equation is transparent: available handling hours multiplied by the chosen occupancy allowance, compared with expected workload plus an exception reserve. It is a scenario model. It does not establish what one offshore coordinator should accomplish everywhere.

## Test peak and recovery scenarios

Model at least three conditions: an ordinary week, a credible peak, and a disruption. The peak might combine a hiring campaign with concentrated interview availability. The disruption might be a system outage, an absent approver, or unusable input. For each scenario, estimate the oldest item, overdue count, and recovery time after arrivals return to normal.

Set a trigger before the peak occurs. Options include narrowing the intake window, borrowing trained coverage, pausing low-priority sourcing, or asking managers to resolve incomplete briefs. The trigger must identify who decides. An offshore coordinator can surface capacity evidence, but should not silently reprioritize candidates or roles when the tradeoff belongs to the hiring owner.

## Use a pilot rather than a promise

Run a defined pilot across enough weeks to include ordinary variability. Freeze the stage definitions during the measurement window or annotate every change. Compare forecast arrivals with actual arrivals, planned workload with actual completed work, and service-window performance with quality and rework. A fast queue with incomplete records is not evidence of adequate capacity.

During the pilot, review a sample of both completed and delayed records. Delays caused by missing manager decisions should not be attributed to coordinator capacity, but they still matter to the candidate experience. Report active processing delay separately from external waiting. That distinction tells the buyer whether to add capacity, repair intake, or improve approval coverage.

## Decision table for buyers

Maintain current capacity when the queue stays inside the service window, quality holds, exception demand remains within reserve, and recovery after a peak is prompt. Redesign intake when incomplete or late requests dominate. Add cross-trained coverage when peaks are brief but material. Consider additional dedicated capacity when sustained, comparable workload exceeds workable hours after avoidable rework has been addressed.

Do not use overtime as the default reserve. Repeated overtime changes the real capacity of the lane and can degrade judgment. Do not compare people using raw output when their role mix differs. Geography is not a capacity variable; process design, skill, tools, work mix, and management support are.

## Limitations and uncertainty

## Reconcile capacity with candidate experience

Capacity is not only an internal cost question. A queue that is technically completed within a weekly target can still impose long, unexplained gaps on candidates. Add candidate-facing checkpoints to the model: acknowledgement, promised next update, actual update, and closure. Measure missed promises separately from total elapsed time. This reveals whether a broad service window is masking preventable uncertainty.

When volume exceeds the plan, protect truthful communication before adding optimistic promises. An approved holding message can state what is known and when the next update will occur, but it should not imply that a hiring decision has been made. Candidates who withdraw should remain visible in the demand history when their records lawfully remain available, because removing them can make an overloaded process appear healthier than it was.

Buyer-side decisions also consume capacity. Track time waiting for brief approval, shortlist review, and interviewer availability by owner category without turning the report into blame. If a coordinator repeatedly reaches the boundary on time but the next decision arrives late, adding another coordinator will not fix the constraint. The model should lead to the narrowest intervention supported by evidence.

Historical arrivals may not represent a new campaign or business cycle. Logged handling time can be distorted by inconsistent pausing or multitasking. Small samples make tail estimates noisy. Process improvements can also invalidate earlier observations. Report the period, sample size, missing-data rate, segmentation rules, and material operating changes beside every forecast.

Any ratio produced through this method is a local estimate that should be reviewed when role mix or service commitments change.

## Buyer checklist

Before approving a staffing level, confirm that the demand record uses one entry rule; work is segmented by observable complexity; waiting is separated from active handling; reserve is explicit; quality and rework are reviewed beside speed; peak recovery is modeled; and the decision owner is named. ## FAQ

### Is utilization the same as productivity?

No. Utilization is a capacity-planning relationship between workload and available time. Productivity also depends on quality, complexity, rework, tools, and outcomes.

### How often should the model be reviewed?

Review it after a pilot, a material role-mix change, a service-window change, or repeated peak failures. A monthly review may suit an active hiring period, but cadence should follow decision need rather than habit.
