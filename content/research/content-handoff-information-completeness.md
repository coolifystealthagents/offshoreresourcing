## Research question and scope

Which information should travel with an article when responsibility moves between an offshore contributor and an onshore reviewer? This desk study translates institutional guidance on communication, records, and human factors into an editorial setting. It evaluates the handoff record, not the nationality or capability of the people involved.

## Methodology

We reviewed official and professional guidance on structured handoffs, records management, plain language, and remote work. Concepts were included only when they could be mapped to an observable article field. The proposed minimum is: current state, completed checks, evidence links, open questions, next requested action, receiver, due time, and escalation owner.

## Evidence-informed interpretation

Structured communication guidance from safety-critical settings supports making the situation, relevant background, assessment, and requested response explicit. Records guidance adds provenance, access, and retention. Plain-language guidance adds a reader test: the receiver should understand what happened and what to do next without decoding private shorthand.

An Offshore Resourcing handoff receipt can therefore remain short. It should link the approved brief and draft, identify material changes, state which claims still need review, and name the precise decision requested. A receipt is complete when the receiving owner can accept, return, or escalate the work using the record.

## Inference limits

Evidence from healthcare and safety settings does not prove the same effect size in editorial production. More fields can create noise, and a template cannot replace access to the actual source or a conversation about a novel exception. The useful inference concerns design: predictable fields reduce reliance on memory and reveal missing decisions.

## Limitations and decision use

No comparative trial was conducted on this site. The appropriate fields vary with claim risk, systems, and team size. Pilot the receipt on five handoffs. Count missing-access events, clarification loops, returned items, and time to the first useful receiver action. Retain fields only when they support a decision or verification step.
