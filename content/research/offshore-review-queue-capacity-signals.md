## Research question

Published September 2, 2026. Contributor throughput does not equal usable delivery when completed work waits for review. Capacity planning should represent both production and acceptance.

## Findings

The clearest signals are review-ready inventory, oldest waiting item, exception share, minutes per check, and decisions missed before their safe deadline.

Teams can use a trigger table to connect thresholds with actions such as narrowing intake, adding qualified review coverage, or changing the sample.

## Practical takeaways

- Measure review-ready work separately from active production.
- Protect decision windows for exceptions and customer-impacting work.
- Reforecast from observed review time after each operating cycle.

## Control table

| Signal | Evidence | Accountable response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review-ready inventory | Queue status history | Queue owner protects review window |
| Oldest waiting item | Created and accepted timestamps | Service owner resolves delay |
| Exception share | Exception register | Manager reserves decision capacity |
| Rework after review | Correction records | Process owner repairs acceptance rules |

## Implementation routine

Start with one current workflow and a short review period. Record the baseline, apply one agreed control, and compare the same measures at the next checkpoint. Contributors can assemble evidence and flag exceptions; managers retain decisions about access, staffing, policy, budget, employment, and customer commitments.
