## Research question

What evidence shows that training has transferred into live support work in a cross-border role? The question is different from whether a person completed modules or passed a quiz. Offshore Resourcing needs a bounded way to assess whether a Philippines based specialist can apply the client's rules, recognize exceptions, and produce evidence in the real workflow.

This review compares training evaluation guidance with role-specific evidence. It uses transfer as the unit: a learned action appears in a live or realistic task under normal constraints. It does not estimate an average transfer rate.

## Completion is not transfer

The Kirkpatrick model separates reaction, learning, behavior, and results. That distinction is useful, even though the model itself does not solve measurement design. A course completion record shows exposure. A knowledge test shows performance under test conditions. Neither proves that the person can use the rule in a live queue with incomplete records and time pressure.

The ILO and OECD both treat skills as related to work context and changing demands. For a client role, transfer evidence should therefore be specific to the work: an approved example, a realistic exception, a written escalation, and a review of the final output. The evaluator should record which rule was used and what uncertainty remained.

## A transfer study for offshore staffing

Start with a small set of representative tasks. Include one normal case, one incomplete case, and one case that must stop for approval. Observe the output, the source used, the explanation, and the handoff. Repeat after a defined interval rather than relying on the first successful attempt.

The review should separate instruction failure from role or system failure. If the example was incomplete, the worker may not have had enough evidence. If the rule changed without an update, the training record is stale. If the action was correct but the handoff lacked an owner, the process boundary needs work. These findings should not be collapsed into a candidate score.

A Filipino specialist can demonstrate transfer without being asked to imitate a client's accent or personality. The evaluation should focus on the task, evidence, judgment boundary, and communication needed for the role. That makes the work sample more relevant and fair.

## Limitations

Training transfer is affected by manager behavior, tools, workload, feedback, and whether the live role matches the training. A realistic exercise still cannot reproduce every customer or policy condition. The evidence does not support claims about national traits, guaranteed productivity, or a fixed onboarding duration.

## Conclusion

Training should be judged by observable use in representative work, not completion alone. For Philippines based support, transfer evidence should include normal work, an exception, a stop point, and a reviewable handoff. That gives a manager a better decision signal while preserving the boundary between learning evidence and future performance.
