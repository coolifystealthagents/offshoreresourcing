## Key stats
- A useful contingency plan names critical work and a safe fallback.
- Evidence gaps should trigger replacement or pause, not unsupported drafting.
- Recovery notes preserve context when work changes hands.
## Key takeaways
- Identify single points of failure.
- Keep a replacement-topic path ready.
- Resume from the last validated gate.
## Contingency map
Use the [offshore content continuity plan](/research/offshore-content-continuity-plan) and record trigger, owner, fallback, and restart check.
| Disruption | Safe response |
| --- | --- |
| Owner absent | Use named backup |
| Source unavailable | Replace or pause |
| Tool failure | Use approved local record |
| Scope change | Rebrief and recheck |
## Methodology and limitations
This plan synthesizes public continuity, quality, research, security, privacy, and accessibility guidance. It does not guarantee uninterrupted operations or replace incident procedures.

## FAQs
### What should never be improvised?
Do not improvise evidence, permissions, publication authority, or claims when a source is missing.
### What proves recovery?
The next owner can locate the brief, evidence, status, and unresolved decisions and pass validation.
