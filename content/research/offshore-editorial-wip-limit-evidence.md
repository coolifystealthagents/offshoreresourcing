## Research question

Can a work-in-progress limit help a Philippines-based content team finish more publishable articles without weakening review? The relevant outcome is accepted work, not drafting activity. A limit may expose congestion, but it cannot repair an unclear brief or create reviewer capacity.

## Evidence scope and method

This desk review combines flow-management guidance with research on task switching and cognitive load. The sources do not test Offshore Resourcing or establish one ideal limit for editorial work. They support a local before-and-after measurement design.

For two comparable periods, record when each item enters brief, draft, review, correction, and release. Before changing the limit, preserve the acceptance test. Then cap active items in the most congested lane and compare completed throughput, elapsed time, review wait, first-pass acceptance, and urgent exceptions. Report queue mix because a week of routine updates is not comparable with a week of evidence-heavy articles.

## What the evidence can show

A useful signal would be lower review waiting and fewer abandoned drafts without a decline in acceptance. Throughput alone is ambiguous: it can rise when articles become shorter or reviewers become less exacting. A visible blocked lane also helps managers distinguish excess intake from slow writing.

The contributor may update states and flag a full lane. The editorial owner chooses the limit, priority exceptions, and any missed commitment. Never reward staff for hiding drafts outside the board.

## Limitations

Local volume is small, article difficulty varies, and learning or staffing changes can confound a comparison. Kanban guidance describes management practices rather than guaranteeing causal effects. Task-switching research often uses controlled tasks unlike long-form editorial work. Findings should therefore be treated as operational evidence, not a universal productivity claim.

## Conclusion

A WIP limit is worth testing when unfinished work and review waits are visible problems. Use a stable quality gate, disclose changes in content mix, and keep an override owner. Retain the limit only if accepted flow improves without hiding work or increasing defects.
