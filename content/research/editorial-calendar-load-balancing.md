## Key stats
- Calendar balance depends on quality-gate capacity, not only publishing dates.
- A reserve protects the schedule from evidence and review exceptions.
- Clustering related work can reduce context switching.
## Key takeaways
- Load the calendar by stage.
- Protect final review windows.
- Rebalance when evidence or ownership changes.
## Planning method
Pair the [editorial calendar capacity planning](/research/editorial-calendar-capacity-planning) view with a daily queue review. Mark each item by stage, effort, dependency, and confidence.
| Signal | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full review lane | Approval bottleneck | Move drafting |
| Empty research lane | Upstream gap | Pull brief |
| High dependency | Fragile date | Add reserve |
## Methodology and limitations
This model draws on public quality, research, search, statistics, privacy, and accessibility guidance. It does not predict demand or assign contractual delivery dates.

## FAQs
### What should move first when capacity changes?
Move work with the weakest evidence or least time-sensitive intent, then record the decision.
### Is a full calendar efficient?
Not necessarily. A small reserve helps absorb rework and urgent evidence checks.
