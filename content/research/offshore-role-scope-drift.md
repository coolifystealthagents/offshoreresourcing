## Finding

Role scope drift is less a writing problem than a decision problem. When a position starts with broad labels such as “general support,” the buyer cannot compare candidates against a stable outcome. The result is often a mismatch between the work described, the work assessed, and the work handed over after selection.

## What the evidence suggests

The ILO and OECD both treat task definition, skills, and working conditions as connected parts of job quality. BLS occupational descriptions similarly separate duties from the knowledge required to perform them. For offshore hiring, that distinction matters: a coordinator who maintains records is not automatically suited to customer-facing judgment or editorial review.

## Decision model

| Scope question | Useful evidence | Hiring implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What outcome is owned? | A measurable service result | Test judgment against the result |
| What decisions are allowed? | Escalation examples | Make authority explicit |
| What work is excluded? | Boundary statement | Avoid accidental role expansion |
| What quality signal matters? | Sample output or service metric | Compare candidates fairly |

## Practical implication

Separate the role’s recurring responsibilities from adjacent work that may be requested later. A clear brief should name the audience, inputs, outputs, decision rights, and escalation owner. This lets an offshore staffing partner source for demonstrated capability rather than a loose title.
