## Research finding

Time to value should measure dependable contribution, not the number of orientation sessions completed. For an offshore hire, useful contribution begins when the person can perform an agreed task, explain exceptions, and request help through the correct owner.

## A balanced measure

| Signal | What it shows | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role knowledge | Understanding of scope | Does not prove execution |
| Supervised output | Ability on a real example | Sample must be representative |
| Independent output | Transfer into normal work | Define acceptable support |
| Exception handling | Judgment under uncertainty | Score explanation, not speed |

Research on organizational socialization from CIPD and SHRM emphasizes role clarity, feedback, and relationships. ISO 10015 similarly frames competence development around organizational needs and evaluation. These sources point to a staged definition of readiness rather than a single start-date milestone.

## Buyer implication

Agree on one or two early outcomes before hiring. For a research support role, that might be a source-backed brief with clear uncertainty markers. For a coordinator, it might be a correctly routed request with a complete record. Keep the measure tied to the role and review it with the client owner.

## Conclusion

Good onboarding reduces ambiguity. It does not promise instant autonomy, and it does not reward busywork as a proxy for value.
