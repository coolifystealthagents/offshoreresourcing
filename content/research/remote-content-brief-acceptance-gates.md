## Key stats

- A complete brief should answer five questions: audience, intent, evidence, format, and owner.
- NIST describes six core Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 functions, a useful reminder to name every control boundary.
- Acceptance before drafting moves ambiguity to the cheapest review point.

## Key takeaways

- Require a one-line decision the article will help the reader make.
- Link each material claim to a source or mark it as a recommendation.
- Assign one accountable approver before the brief enters the queue.

## Acceptance gate matrix

| Gate | Evidence | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent | Query and reader decision | Strategist |
| Scope | Included and excluded questions | Editor |
| Evidence | Source register with dates | Researcher |
| Delivery | Format, links, and deadline | Coordinator |
