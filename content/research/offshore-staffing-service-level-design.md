## Decision in brief

Set service levels only after defining the work unit, valid inputs, acceptance evidence, clock rules, quality checks, exceptions, and decision owners. A fast response target can improve visibility, but it can also reward premature closure, duplicate handling, shallow review, or avoidance of difficult cases. Pair time measures with quality and queue evidence.

This method is intended for buyers planning Philippines-based offshore support across recruiting coordination, administration, customer operations, reporting, and other repeatable lanes. It does not prescribe universal targets or determine employment, wage, privacy, professional, or customer obligations. Those requirements must come from the buyer's contracts, policies, risk owners, and qualified advisers.

## Define the service before the metric

Write the service as an observable exchange. State what enters the queue, who may submit it, the minimum required information, what the support role may do, what output counts as complete, who accepts it, and where the record lives. Include one normal example and one exception.

Avoid using a broad job title as the service definition. "Recruitment coordinator" might cover interview scheduling, candidate follow-up, records, reporting, or offer administration. Each has different inputs, authority, sensitivity, and consequences. Measure the bounded lane rather than the person in the abstract.

Separate service commitments from individual performance management. A missed target can arise from demand spikes, missing inputs, unavailable approvers, system failure, poor instructions, or execution. The service record should preserve those causes rather than converting every miss into a judgment about a worker.

## Choose the work unit

The denominator determines whether a metric can be interpreted. A ticket, candidate, interview, report, transaction, or customer conversation may contain several actions. Define when a unit begins, splits, merges, reopens, and ends. Prevent easy duplicate units from inflating throughput.

Segment materially different work. A routine scheduling confirmation should not hide a complex accessibility request or conflicting panel availability. Use a small number of categories with written definitions. Review classification consistency before comparing results.

For knowledge work, milestones and acceptance evidence may be more useful than units per hour. Do not manufacture a precision measure when the output varies substantially. A decision brief can be assessed for required fields, source traceability, unresolved questions, and timely handoff.

## Set valid-input rules

A service clock should not start as though work were ready when mandatory information is missing. Define the minimum input and a visible status for incomplete requests. The support role can acknowledge receipt, identify the gap, and route it to the owner without pretending the task is in active production.

Use reason codes for common gaps, but retain a short note for material context. Track incomplete demand because it consumes time and may reveal a broken intake. Do not exclude it from every report and then claim the service has no upstream problem.

Name who can waive an input, especially when urgency is asserted. The offshore role should not silently accept risk or invent business facts to keep the clock green.

## Define the clocks

Response time measures the interval to a defined first action, not an automated status unless that is the promised action. Resolution time measures the interval to accepted closure. Active work time, waiting time, and end-to-end elapsed time answer different questions. Record the states and transitions used in calculation.

Specify timezone, service days, coverage hours, holidays, pause conditions, reopen rules, and daylight-saving effects in the buyer's region. The Philippines does not observe daylight saving time, while buyer locations may change their offset. Publish schedules with explicit zones rather than relying on abbreviations.

Do not pause the clock merely because responsibility is inconvenient. A valid pause needs a defined dependency, owner, timestamp, and next action. Report buyer waits and provider waits separately where useful, while retaining total customer elapsed time.

## Pair speed with acceptance quality

Define the evidence required for completion. A scheduled interview may require confirmed participants, correct zones, approved channel, calendar record, candidate message, and exception note. A report may require named source records, reconciliation, review date, and owner acceptance.

Sample completed work using a stated method. Record material defects, minor corrections, reopened items, duplicate actions, and missing evidence. Keep categories stable long enough to learn, then version changes. One overall accuracy percentage can hide rare but consequential errors.

Use acceptance at first review carefully. It can expose rework, but reviewers may apply criteria differently. Calibrate with shared examples and periodically compare classifications. If reviewers disagree, repair the definition before using the score in a consequential decision.

## Measure the queue, not only closed work

Throughput can look healthy while old, difficult items accumulate. Report open volume by age, risk, work type, owner, and reason. Identify the oldest actionable items and blocked exceptions. Little's Law can relate average work in progress, throughput, and cycle time in a stable system, but real service queues may violate its assumptions.

Track arrival rate and demand mix alongside completions. A target based on a quiet month may fail during a seasonal peak. Capacity planning should include leave, training, meetings, quality review, outages, and manager availability rather than treating every scheduled hour as productive time.

Set a work-in-progress approach that fits the process. Too many simultaneous items increase switching and hide waiting. An explicit priority owner should decide which item moves when demand exceeds capacity.

## Design exceptions and escalation

List events the offshore support role may resolve, must refer, and must stop. Examples include missing authorization, conflicting records, privacy concerns, financial approvals, threats, professional judgment, or a customer commitment outside policy. Assign an escalation channel, response expectation, backup owner, and safe holding action.

Measure escalation quality as well as count. A useful escalation contains the source, observed issue, work already completed, impact, deadline, and requested decision without copying unnecessary sensitive data. A high count may indicate good detection, a broken process, or expanding scope. Interpret it with reason and outcome.

Manager response time belongs in the service model. If the buyer retains consequential decisions, the queue depends on an available buyer owner. State what happens when that owner is absent.

## Avoid metric gaming

Every target changes behavior. A first-response target may produce empty acknowledgements. A closure target may produce premature resolution. A throughput target may favor easy work. An accuracy target may suppress reporting of errors. Review predictable counter-behaviors before launch.

Use a compact set of balancing signals: end-to-end time, accepted work, returns by reason, open aging, material exceptions, and reviewer effort. Not every signal needs a contractual threshold. Some are diagnostic and should trigger a conversation or sample review.

Do not combine unrelated measures into a single opaque score. Decision makers should see tradeoffs. If faster service coincides with more reopened work, the separate series makes the change visible.

## Set targets from evidence

Begin with a bounded baseline under the proposed scope. Record demand, mix, active and waiting time, quality, exceptions, and review effort. Remove known training or system-test artifacts only with a documented reason. Use ranges and distributions rather than relying solely on averages.

Set an initial service objective, review date, and conditions. Distinguish an internal planning objective from a contractual commitment. A percentile may describe consistency better than a mean, but it still needs enough representative observations.

Do not promise a target copied from another company or a provider's standard package without checking the workflow. The correct threshold depends on customer impact, downstream dependencies, staffing, coverage, input quality, and the cost of error.

## Connect remedies to control

If contractual credits or remedies apply, link them to measures the responsible party can influence and specify exclusions narrowly. Define data source, calculation, reporting, dispute, force-majeure treatment, planned maintenance, and repeated failure. Credits do not repair customer harm or replace a recovery plan.

Operational remedies may be more useful early: root-cause review, instruction repair, added sampling, temporary volume reduction, access change, capacity adjustment, or manager action. Name the authority and evidence for returning to normal.

Avoid automatic penalties that encourage concealment or unsafe speed. Legal and procurement owners should review contractual mechanisms, while operations verifies that the measure reflects the actual service.

## Run a measured pilot

Test the service definition with ordinary work, a missing input, an exception, a reopened item, and a handoff near the edge of coverage. Use limited access and approved data. Verify timestamps, states, acceptance, and reports rather than reading only the summary dashboard.

Ask whether another authorized reviewer can reproduce the measure from source records. Check that private notes and worker surveillance are not being collected merely because they are easy to count. The Philippine Data Privacy Act framework requires legitimate purpose, transparency, proportionality, and safeguards for personal-data processing.

At the review date, decide whether to adopt, revise, narrow, or reject the service level. Preserve the version and effective date so historical results are not judged against later definitions.

## Limitations

No service level proves overall value, customer satisfaction, lawful processing, or individual performance. Measurement can be distorted by changing demand, selection bias, inconsistent reviewers, small samples, outages, and unrecorded work. Statistical relationships do not by themselves show cause.

The framework requires local validation. Revisit measures after role, system, volume, coverage, customer commitment, reviewer, or access changes. High-stakes decisions need evidence beyond one dashboard.

## Buyer conclusion

A useful service level makes the work legible. It defines the unit, inputs, clock, acceptance, queue, exceptions, and owners before setting a target. Speed remains visible, but it is read beside quality and unresolved work. That gives an Offshore Resourcing buyer a better basis for designing a role, comparing proposals, and reviewing a pilot without rewarding the wrong behavior.
