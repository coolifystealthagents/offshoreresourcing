## Key stats

- The Philippines applies statutory wage floors by region rather than one national private-sector minimum.
- Total operating cost includes recruitment, equipment, management time, security, benefits, replacement coverage, and provider margin.
- Exchange rates change, so budgets should keep the source currency and verification date.

## Key takeaways

- Compare like-for-like role scope, coverage hours, controls, and replacement terms.
- Treat a low headline rate as incomplete until inclusions and exclusions are documented.
- Keep hiring, pay, classification, tax, and privacy decisions with qualified owners.

## A practical cost model

| Cost layer | What to include | Owner check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worker compensation | Base pay, differentials, statutory items | Current regional and role assumptions |
| Delivery | Recruitment, equipment, workspace, connectivity | Named inclusions and replacement terms |
| Management | Training, QA, meetings, escalation time | Hours required from the client team |
| Risk controls | Security, privacy, continuity, legal review | Evidence and accountable owner |

Use the [workforce planning support page](/services/workforce-planning-support) to define the role before requesting a quote. The [recruitment administration page](/services/recruitment-administration) explains a nearby support lane.

## How to compare quotes

Ask each provider to quote the same role brief. Record currency, tax treatment, coverage, equipment, benefits, leave, replacement, termination, security, and management assumptions. A fair comparison should show both monthly cash cost and the client time needed to operate the role.
