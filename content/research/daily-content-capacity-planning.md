## Key stats

- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects writer and author employment to grow 4% from 2024 to 2034.
- A 12-article queue needs explicit writing, research, review, and correction capacity.
- Capacity is a control variable, not a reason to lower evidence standards.

## Key takeaways

- Count review hours separately from drafting hours.
- Reserve space for factual corrections and refreshes.
- Stop intake when the next article cannot receive a named reviewer.

## Capacity model

| Work unit | Measure | Release gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Source notes per brief | Ten real sources recorded |
| Drafting | Articles in progress | One owner per article |
| Review | Claims and links checked | Reviewer sign-off |
| Refresh | Aging pages sampled | Next action assigned |

## Operating method

Use the [editorial calendar capacity planning](/research/editorial-calendar-capacity-planning) page for the queue, then link each assignment to a brief and evidence record. The [research brief quality gates](/research/research-brief-quality-gates) page defines the minimum acceptance checks. A daily stand-up should report only ready, blocked, and released work.
