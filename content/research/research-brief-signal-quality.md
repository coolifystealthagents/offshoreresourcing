## Finding

A research brief becomes decision-ready when a reader can see the question, the evidence, the uncertainty, and the consequence. A long source list is not enough. The brief must show why each source is relevant and what action the evidence supports.

## Quality signals

| Signal | Strong brief | Weak brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question | Narrow and answerable | Broad topic label |
| Evidence | Claim-linked sources | Decorative links |
| Uncertainty | Limits are named | Confidence is implied |
| Decision | Consequence is explicit | Reader must infer it |

The U.S. National Academies, Pew Research Center, and the UK Government Analysis Function all emphasize transparent methods and limits. These principles are useful for offshore research support because they make review less dependent on familiarity with the researcher’s work.

## Role implication

When hiring for research support, assess whether candidates can summarize a source accurately, distinguish fact from interpretation, and flag what the evidence cannot establish. Those behaviors are more valuable than an impressive volume of collected material.

## Conclusion

The best brief reduces the next decision’s ambiguity. Its structure should make a reviewer faster and more skeptical in the right places.
