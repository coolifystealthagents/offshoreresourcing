## Research question and scope

What makes an editorial decision record usable by a colleague who was not present when the decision was made? This review covers routine content decisions and handoffs in distributed teams. It does not claim that written records can replace every discussion or professional judgment.

## Methodology

We synthesized primary guidance on records, decision documentation, internal control, and remote work. We identified fields that recur across those sources, then tested them against a practical handoff: can the next owner identify the decision, basis, authority, effect, and next action without guessing? We did not observe company communications.

## Findings

A usable record states the question, decision, accountable owner, date, evidence considered, rejected or deferred options when relevant, affected content, next action, and review trigger. Links need stable labels and access appropriate to the next owner.

The amount of context should match the consequence and reversibility of the choice. A routine metadata correction needs less explanation than approval of a disputed factual claim. Templates help with recall, but mandatory fields that never affect action create noise and make important qualifications harder to find.

## Inference boundaries

A complete-looking record does not prove that the decision was sound or authorised. Written detail also cannot establish that every recipient understood it. Follow-up questions, later reversals, and exceptions provide evidence about whether the record worked in practice.

## Limitations and practical use

The sources address several domains, so applying them to editorial work is an inference. Pilot a short template on high-friction handoffs, interview the receiving owner about missing context, and revise the template before requiring it across the queue.
