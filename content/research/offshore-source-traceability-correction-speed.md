## Research question

Does recording the source, access date, supported claim, and relevant location help a distributed team investigate factual concerns faster? Traceability should make reasoning reproducible. It should not turn the presence of a citation into proof that the wording is accurate.

## Evidence scope and method

Standards for data provenance, records, reproducibility, and evidence review support traceable inputs. They do not quantify the benefit for Offshore Resourcing articles. Build a retrospective sample of correction investigations and code whether a usable claim record existed. Compare time to locate evidence, time to editorial decision, number of clarification messages, and final disposition. Separate broken links, changed facts, interpretation disputes, and unsupported claims.

## Decision rules

A record counts as usable only when another reviewer can locate the passage and understand the relationship to the published sentence. Home-page links and unexplained source lists do not qualify. Researchers may document the evidence and limitation; the accountable editor decides correction language and publication timing.

## Limitations

Older articles may have weaker records and slower corrections for unrelated reasons. Easy claims are more likely to receive neat citations. Investigation time may be missing from chat-based cases. The study can indicate association and operational usefulness, not causal certainty.

## Conclusion

Pilot claim-level traceability on the highest-risk assertions rather than every sentence. Expand only if reviewers can resolve concerns faster while maintaining source quality and appropriately cautious wording.
