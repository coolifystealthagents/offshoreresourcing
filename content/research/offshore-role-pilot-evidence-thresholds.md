## Research question

What evidence should a manager require before expanding a small offshore role pilot? The useful unit of analysis is not whether the participant appeared busy or whether stakeholders felt optimistic. It is whether a defined workflow moved from approved input to accepted output with visible exceptions, appropriate access, and timely management decisions.

## Evidence model

A pilot record should separate four layers. The first is workload: eligible items offered, completed, held, and excluded. The second is quality: accepted outputs, returned outputs, defect reasons, and reviewer agreement. The third is control performance: permissions used, exceptions stopped, approvals requested, and decisions received. The fourth is operating burden: training time, reviewer minutes, clarification loops, and work displaced elsewhere.

| Decision | Minimum evidence | Warning signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expand | Stable accepted output across ordinary and exception cases | Growth proposed after only easy items |
| Repeat | Useful result but an instruction or access gap remains | Same pilot repeated without a named change |
| Revise | Defect pattern points to a correctable process weakness | Individual blamed without testing the workflow |
| Stop | Work is unsuitable, unsafe, or too dependent on scarce judgment | Sunk cost used as the reason to continue |

## Measurement approach

Managers can define thresholds before live work begins. Examples include the number and mix of items to observe, which defects require immediate pause, the maximum age of an unanswered escalation, and the reviewer capacity available. Thresholds should be interpreted together. A high acceptance rate is weak evidence if reviewers sampled only simple items or privately completed missing steps.

The pilot should include at least one incomplete request and one exception when this can be done safely. Those cases test whether boundaries work under pressure. The team should never manufacture a risky live event merely to fill a sample; a controlled fictional example can test the stop and escalation path.

## Interpretation limits

A pilot is a local operating test, not proof of general productivity, employment fit, legal compliance, or long-term financial return. Small samples are sensitive to task mix and reviewer behavior. Early performance also includes learning effects that may not represent steady state. Managers should preserve the observed counts and reasons rather than compressing the result into a single score.

## Governance implications

The manager who owns the workflow should approve the threshold, sample, and expansion decision. Security and privacy owners should review any new access. Employment, compensation, contractual, legal, and customer commitments remain with authorized specialists. A coordinator may maintain the evidence record but should not decide that silence or workload pressure permits expansion.

## Practical application

Write a one-page pilot charter before assigning work. List the workflow, eligible volume, accepted examples, system permissions, reviewer windows, pause conditions, evidence fields, and decision date. At midpoint, correct only issues that prevent a fair test and record every change. At close, choose expand, repeat, revise, or stop and name the evidence supporting that choice.
