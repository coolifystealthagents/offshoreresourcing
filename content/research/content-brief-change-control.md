## Key stats
- Unrecorded brief changes create mismatches between intent, evidence, and draft.
- A change record should state what changed, why, and who approved it.
- Material scope changes deserve a fresh uniqueness and source check.
## Key takeaways
- Version the brief before changing its core question.
- Recheck sources when claims or audience change.
- Preserve the old decision for auditability.
## Change routine
Use the [research brief version control](/research/research-brief-version-control) practice. Label changes as editorial, evidence, scope, or release changes and re-run only the affected gates.
| Change | Recheck |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Slug and intent |
| Claim | Source and wording |
| Audience | Usefulness and examples |
| Date | Metadata and freshness |
## Methodology and limitations
This routine is derived from public research, quality, privacy, search, and accessibility guidance. It is a lightweight editorial control, not a formal change-management standard.

## FAQs
### What makes a change material?
A change is material when it alters the reader decision, headline evidence, target audience, or article scope.
### Can editors change wording without approval?
They can make low-risk clarity edits. Log any edit that changes meaning or evidence.
