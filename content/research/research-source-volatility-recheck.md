## Research question and scope

How should a daily research team decide when an approved source needs another check? This desk study concerns factual claims whose truth or presentation may change over time. It does not create a universal expiry period and does not replace specialist legal, tax, employment, security, or financial review.

## Methodology

We compared publisher update practices and institutional guidance on data quality, vulnerability information, labour statistics, and web documentation. We classify volatility by change mechanism rather than prestige: law or policy amendment, frequently revised dataset, live product documentation, periodic statistical release, or stable conceptual reference.

## Finding and application

A source can be authoritative and still become stale. The claim register should record the exact supported statement, source owner, source date, access date, expected change trigger, and reviewer. Recheck timing should follow consequence and volatility. A current price, regulation, officeholder, or software instruction requires a tighter check than a stable definition.

For Offshore Resourcing research, publication readiness should include a final check of high-volatility claims and links. If the evidence changed, update the claim and its interpretation rather than only replacing the access date. If the source disappeared, find an authoritative replacement or narrow the claim.

## Inference limits

No interval guarantees accuracy. Publishers may change a page without a visible date, and stable pages can contain errors. Link availability is not evidence that a passage still supports the sentence. Automated checks can identify failures but cannot reliably judge claim fit.

## Limitations and decision use

This framework has not been validated against correction rates on this site. A team may misclassify volatility, and the cost of rechecking differs by subject. Pilot three bands, high, medium, and low, while preserving the rationale. Review corrections and missed changes after 30 days before standardizing any interval.
