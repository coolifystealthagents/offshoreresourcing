## Research question

How can an offshore research team distinguish a report that remains useful from one whose evidence has decayed? Decay can mean a changed statistic, withdrawn guidance, broken link, changed definition, or new evidence that alters the conclusion. The question matters for a daily publishing routine because adding new articles without maintaining old claims can create an uneven library. The unit is a published claim with an owner and review condition.

## Method and evidence scope

This report draws on records management, data governance, risk management, and evidence synthesis guidance. It proposes a maintenance model and does not claim that publication age alone predicts accuracy. Each claim is considered by volatility, consequence, source stability, and replacement difficulty. The model distinguishes a source being unavailable from a source being contradicted; those events require different editorial responses.

## Findings

Maintenance works best when the original brief records why a source mattered. A link without a claim relationship cannot be triaged efficiently. Stable definitions may need only link checks, while market figures or policy descriptions need substantive revalidation. An offshore researcher can support maintenance by recording retrieval date, source owner, period, and a short “what would change this conclusion” note.

## Decay signals

Useful signals include a broken URL, a changed page title, a revised dataset, a new edition, a changed denominator, a superseded policy, or a correction notice. Traffic decline is not an evidence signal. A source can remain correct after attention falls. Likewise, a new page is not automatically a reason to rewrite. The reviewer should compare the old claim with the new evidence and preserve the decision trail.

## Operating ownership

The researcher can monitor source conditions and propose a recheck. The editor owns whether the public article needs correction. A subject or system owner may need to approve claims involving access, privacy, or security. The role should not silently change publication dates or disguise an update as a new finding. A maintenance queue should identify the decision, evidence, and owner rather than merely list URLs.

## Measurement

Track claims reviewed on schedule, claims requiring change, broken-source rate, and time from signal to decision. Segment by topic and volatility. A high change rate can indicate good monitoring rather than poor original research. Review a sample of claims with no signal to test whether the monitoring rules miss meaningful decay. Keep an audit note concise so maintenance remains feasible in a daily routine.

## Limitations

No monitoring process sees every change, especially when sources are withdrawn or inaccessible. Automated link checks cannot judge meaning. The model also does not set a universal review interval. A claim may become more important after publication because readers reuse it in a new context. Maintenance therefore requires judgment and a visible correction path.

## Conclusion

Evidence decay is a relationship problem: the team must know which source supports which claim and who decides when the relationship no longer holds. Offshore research teams should record provenance, volatility, and change signals, then route substantive decisions to an editor. That keeps a growing library useful without treating age or traffic as a substitute for evidence review.

## Maintenance triage

Triage should start with the claim, not the URL. A broken source supporting a minor definition can wait for a routine update, while a working source supporting a consequential statistic may need immediate review if the underlying period has changed. Record the reason for the priority and the owner. This keeps a growing research library manageable and stops link-checking from becoming a substitute for editorial judgment.

## Correction path

When evidence no longer supports a published sentence, choose among correcting, narrowing, annotating, or removing it. Preserve the reason and the date of the decision. Do not silently re-date an article to make an old finding appear new. For offshore content work, an explicit correction path also teaches researchers what signals matter and gives editors a way to distinguish a routine refresh from a new research contribution.

## FAQ

### Is a broken link proof that an article is wrong?

No. It is a signal to inspect the claim and find an authoritative replacement if needed.

### Who decides whether to correct public copy?

The accountable editor, with subject or system owners involved where appropriate.
