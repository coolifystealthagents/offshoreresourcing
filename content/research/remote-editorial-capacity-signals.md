## Research finding

Editorial capacity is a function of work mix, not a fixed number of pages per person. Source complexity, stakeholder feedback, accessibility needs, and revision risk can make two apparently similar assignments require very different effort.

## A better capacity view

| Variable | Why it matters | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | Determines volume | Historical request pattern |
| Complexity | Changes research effort | Topic and source profile |
| Review load | Adds elapsed time | Number of decision-makers |
| Context switching | Reduces focus | Concurrent work categories |

Operations research from the ILO, OECD, and PMI supports looking at workload and constraints together. Capacity estimates should also include time for clarification and correction, because an apparently full schedule can hide unresolved work.

## Offshore team implication

Ask a resourcing partner to describe capacity in terms of role scope and work characteristics. A research specialist, coordinator, and editor may each contribute to the same article while owning different risks. Sizing the team by title alone conceals those dependencies.

## Conclusion

Capacity is credible when its assumptions are visible. Revisit the model when demand or complexity changes, not only when a deadline is missed.
