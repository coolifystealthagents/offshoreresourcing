## Research question

Do handoffs that name the article state, unresolved evidence, next owner, due time, and acceptance condition move through a distributed editorial queue with less delay? The practical concern is not whether a template is filled in. It is whether the receiving person can take the correct next action without reconstructing context in chat.

## Evidence scope and method

Research on team coordination, shift handovers, and information continuity supports explicit transfer records, but it does not establish a universal editorial template. Offshore Resourcing should treat the relationship as a local operational hypothesis. Score a consecutive sample of handoffs for five required fields, then compare complete and incomplete records on time to next action, clarification requests, returns, and post-publication corrections. Stratify by article difficulty and handoff direction.

## Interpretation

A shorter wait among complete records would be useful only if acceptance and correction rates remain stable. Completeness may be a proxy for experienced staff, easier articles, or quieter days. Managers should review missing fields as process evidence, not automatically as individual performance failure. The handoff owner records facts; the editor retains authority over claims and release.

## Limitations

The sample will be observational, local, and vulnerable to selection bias. Scoring can drift unless reviewers calibrate examples. A record can be technically complete yet misleading, and urgent work may receive both better handoffs and faster attention. Results therefore describe this workflow and period, not all remote teams.

## Conclusion

Test a small mandatory field set for two comparable production cycles. Keep it if complete handoffs show less avoidable waiting without lower quality, and revise it when fields create clerical work without changing a decision.
