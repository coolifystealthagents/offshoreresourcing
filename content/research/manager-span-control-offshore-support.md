## Research question

How should manager capacity affect the review design for a Philippines based offshore support role? The question is narrower than asking for an ideal number of direct reports. Offshore Resourcing needs to know whether the named client owner can make decisions, sample work, and respond to exceptions at the required pace.

This is a literature and control review. It uses management research for concepts about span and coordination, then applies those concepts to observable support work. It does not claim that any fixed ratio predicts performance.

## What span of control misses

Headcount alone says little about review load. Two roles may each have ten recurring tasks, but one may be self-checking and low consequence while the other needs frequent judgment and customer-sensitive escalation. O*NET's separation of work activities and work context helps explain why titles and counts are poor substitutes for work analysis.

Review load depends on the number of items, the variation in those items, the cost of an error, the amount of manager judgment, and the quality of the records arriving at the role. It also depends on maturity. A new role may need close sampling while an established role needs exception review and periodic calibration.

The relevant unit is a manager decision opportunity. Count approvals, escalations, sampled items, unresolved questions, and changes to instructions over a defined period. A manager who owns five people may be overloaded if each person sends frequent ambiguous cases. A manager with more people may have a lighter load when inputs are complete and decisions are rare.

## Implications for Philippines support

A useful role plan names one accountable manager, a backup, and review moments. The manager should see a small sample of normal work, every high-impact exception, and trends that indicate a rule or source problem. The offshore staff member should not have to guess whether an unusual request is safe to complete.

The plan should also test the owner's response time. A simple fictional case can show whether a question waits because the owner is unavailable, because the evidence is incomplete, or because the authority boundary is unclear. Those are different problems. Adding more staff does not repair a missing decision path.

Managers should review the review system itself. If the sample is always chosen from easy cases, quality evidence is biased. If every case requires manager sign-off, the role may be designed as an extension of the manager rather than a support lane. The right balance depends on risk, reversibility, and the maturity of the work.

## Limitations

There is no reliable universal span-of-control benchmark for offshore support. Management research often studies organizations with different structures and goals. The ILO evidence on Philippine online work describes work arrangements, not manager capacity. A model built from one client's queue should not be generalized to another.

Review volume is also not the same as good management. A manager can review many items and still miss a systemic issue. Conversely, a small number of well-chosen checks can provide useful evidence. The measure must remain connected to decisions the owner actually makes.

## Conclusion

The right question is not how many offshore staff a manager can supervise in theory. It is whether the manager has enough capacity to resolve exceptions, review representative work, and update the role boundary. A Philippines support plan should measure those obligations directly and change the review rhythm when the work mix changes.
