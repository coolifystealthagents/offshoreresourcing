## Key stats
- Access should match the work a person performs.
- Reviewable ownership matters as much as initial provisioning.
- Removing unused access reduces avoidable operational exposure.
## Key takeaways
- Use least privilege for research systems.
- Separate publishing authority from drafting access.
- Review access on a defined cadence.
## Access routine
Start with the [offshore content access review](/research/offshore-content-access-review). Record role, system, permission, approver, review date, and removal condition.
| Control | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need | Why is access required? | Role note |
| Scope | What can the user change? | Permission list |
| Review | When is it checked? | Review record |
| Exit | What ends access? | Offboarding rule |
## Methodology and limitations
This editorial control uses public security, privacy, quality, accessibility, and research guidance. It does not replace an organization's risk assessment or identity provider controls.

## FAQs
### Who approves publishing access?
The person accountable for the publication boundary should approve it, with a documented reason.
### How often should access be reviewed?
Set a cadence that reflects sensitivity and change rate, and review immediately after role changes.
