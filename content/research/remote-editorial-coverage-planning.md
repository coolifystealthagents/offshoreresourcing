## Key stats
- Coverage planning should expose handoff gaps, not only headcount.
- A named backup reduces the risk of a single unavailable reviewer.
- Daily capacity is more useful when measured by completed quality gates.
## Key takeaways
- Map work by stage and owner.
- Protect fact checking and final review capacity.
- Keep a small reserve for exceptions.
## Coverage model
Build a stage map from intake to publication and compare it with the [distributed content coverage model](/research/distributed-content-coverage-model). Track primary owner, backup, service window, and escalation path.
| Stage | Primary control | Backup signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Brief accepted | Queue owner |
| Research | Sources logged | Research reviewer |
| Draft | Structure complete | Editor |
| Publish | Checks passed | Release owner |
## Methodology and limitations
The model combines public quality, research, accessibility, security, and content guidance. It does not prescribe staffing levels or guarantee delivery volume.

## FAQs
### What is the first planning input?
List the stages, quality gates, and expected service windows before estimating people or hours.
### How should a coverage gap be handled?
Pause the affected gate, name the owner, and re-sequence work that does not depend on it.
