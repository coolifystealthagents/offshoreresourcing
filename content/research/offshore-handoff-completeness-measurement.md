## Research question

Published September 2, 2026. A handoff is complete only when the receiving owner can take the next authorised action without reconstructing context from private messages.

## Findings

Completeness can be sampled using required fields: source record, work performed, result, open question, due time, next owner, and acknowledgement.

Teams should distinguish missing information from incorrect information because each points to a different repair in intake, instruction, or review.

## Practical takeaways

- Define completeness from the receiver's next action.
- Sample ordinary and exception handoffs across owners.
- Use defects to repair the handoff template, not merely the individual record.

## Control table

| Signal | Evidence | Accountable response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Required field present | Handoff sample | Sender corrects before transfer |
| Receiver acknowledged | Shared record timestamp | Queue owner follows up |
| Exception clearly stated | Exception record | Decision owner responds |
| Reopened after handoff | Queue history | Process owner finds the missing control |

## Implementation routine

Start with one current workflow and a short review period. Record the baseline, apply one agreed control, and compare the same measures at the next checkpoint. Contributors can assemble evidence and flag exceptions; managers retain decisions about access, staffing, policy, budget, employment, and customer commitments.
