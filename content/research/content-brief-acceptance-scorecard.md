## Key stats

- Google recommends asking Who, How, and Why about content creation.
- A brief with five scored gates makes missing evidence visible before drafting.
- A zero on audience, evidence, or intent is a release blocker.

## Key takeaways

- Score the decision the article supports, not just the keyword.
- Require source URLs before assigning a writer.
- Keep rejected briefs separate from accepted topics.

## Scorecard

| Gate | Pass condition | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | One reader and job to be done | Audience sentence |
| Intent | One primary question | Search-intent note |
| Evidence | Ten credible starting sources | Source register |
| Differentiation | A useful original angle | Comparison or data plan |
| Ownership | Writer and reviewer named | Assignment record |

## Operating method

Use the [research brief version control](/research/research-brief-version-control) page to preserve revisions. A brief can move to drafting only when all five gates pass. For sensitive claims, add a second reviewer and record the method in the [source verification workflow](/research/source-verification-workflow).
