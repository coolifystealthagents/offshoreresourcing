## Overlap is smaller than the clock suggests

A team may advertise four hours of overlap and use less than two for collaborative work. Meetings, handoffs, and interruptions consume the window. Coverage planning should estimate usable overlap rather than total shared hours.

## Define the window

Record the shift start and end, the onshore working hours, and the time zone difference including daylight saving changes. The window is the intersection, not the full shift.

Add the fixed commitments inside the window: standing meetings, one-to-one reviews, and scheduled handoffs.

## Subtract the fixed load

Count the minutes in the window that are already committed. The remainder is the flexible overlap available for live questions, escalations, and joint work.

Report the fraction, not just the hours. Two flexible hours inside a four-hour window is a different capacity from four hours with no meetings.

## Estimate interruptions

Use a short observation sample to estimate how often scheduled collaborative time is interrupted by urgent calls or queue spikes. Record the interruption rate separately.

Do not subtract an assumed percentage without evidence. A measured rate from two or three weeks is enough for planning.

## Use the result

If flexible overlap is thin, move asynchronous work outside the window and protect the overlap for decisions. If the window is adequate but fragmented, reduce recurring meetings.

## Limitations

The method measures available time, not productivity. It cannot capture the value of a short conversation that prevents a day of rework.
