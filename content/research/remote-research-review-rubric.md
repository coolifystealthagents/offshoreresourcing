## Key stats
- A rubric turns broad quality language into observable checks.
- Evidence quality and reader usefulness should be scored separately.
- Review notes are most useful when tied to a section or claim.
## Key takeaways
- Review against the brief, not personal preference.
- Separate blocking defects from polish.
- Calibrate reviewers with examples.
## Rubric
Pair this rubric with the [offshore team quality scorecard](/research/offshore-team-quality-scorecard).
| Dimension | Pass signal | Blocker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence | Claims trace to sources | Unsupported central claim |
| Structure | Reader can scan and act | Missing required section |
| Clarity | Terms are defined | Ambiguous recommendation |
| Integrity | Scope is honest | Misleading comparison |
## Methodology and limitations
The rubric combines public research, quality, search, accessibility, privacy, and plain-language guidance. It is an editorial aid and not a substitute for specialist review.

## FAQs
### Who owns the score?
The assigned reviewer owns the assessment, while the article owner resolves findings.
### Should scores be averaged?
Avoid hiding a blocker in an average. Report each dimension and the highest-severity finding.
