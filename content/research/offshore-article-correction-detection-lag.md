## Research question

How long does it take a daily content operation to detect and resolve different kinds of live article defects? A correction record can reveal weak verification and monitoring points, but only when reader impact and defect class remain visible.

## Methodology and scope

For each confirmed correction, record publication time, discovery time, reporter, defect type, affected routes, impact, containment, approval, correction time, deployment identity, and final recheck. Report detection and resolution intervals by class. Include zero-correction releases in the denominator and audit a sample for unlogged quiet edits.

Quality, incident, accessibility, and web guidance supports defined checks and evidence of correction. These sources do not supply benchmarks for Offshore Resourcing. The proposed taxonomy and review cadence are our operational synthesis.

## Interpretation

Separate factual defects from broken assets, wrong dates, metadata errors, navigation failures, and stylistic preferences. Fast resolution does not excuse slow detection or repeated recurrence. A severe claim correction and a low-impact typo should not be averaged into one quality number.

## Limitations

Undetected defects are absent by definition. Historical logs may be incomplete, and deployment time can differ from publication time. Small counts make averages unstable. The analysis cannot attribute a defect to an individual without examining the brief, source record, review, and release controls.

## Conclusion

Use detection lag to improve the point at which each defect should have become visible. Keep high-impact cases separate, preserve before-and-after evidence, and require an authorized owner for public claim changes.
