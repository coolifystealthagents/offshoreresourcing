## Key stats
- Readiness is a set of passed gates, not a subjective confidence statement.
- Evidence, structure, links, metadata, and build checks should be recorded separately.
- A failed central gate means the article is not ready.
## Key takeaways
- Check family ownership and uniqueness first.
- Resolve factual and structural blockers before polish.
- Record the exact validation evidence.
## Scorecard
Apply the [research brief quality gates](/research/research-brief-quality-gates) and record each result.
| Gate | Pass signal |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Unique Research slug and title |
| Evidence | Sources support material claims |
| Structure | Required sections and links exist |
| Technical | Content and build checks pass |
## Methodology and limitations
This scorecard combines public research, quality, search, accessibility, privacy, and security guidance. It is a release control, not a claim of live-site behavior.

## FAQs
### What should be recorded for a pass?
Record commands, dates, material checks, and the exact files or slugs reviewed.
### Does a pass mean the article is live?
No. It means the repository content passed its defined publication checks. Deployment is a separate boundary.
