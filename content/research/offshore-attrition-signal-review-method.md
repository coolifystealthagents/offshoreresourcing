## The purpose of the review

Attrition is expensive to discover at the resignation. An early signal review looks for changes in work and communication that warrant a conversation, not a prediction of who will leave.

The method is deliberately modest. It combines observable work indicators with a manager check-in and treats both as inputs to a decision, not proof.

## Select the indicators

Use indicators that are already recorded: queue ownership, handoff completeness, rework rate, response time, and participation in scheduled reviews.

Record the baseline for each person over a stable period, then review changes over two to four weeks. A single difficult week is not a signal.

## Add the conversation

A work signal can mean many things: unclear priorities, blocked access, a personal issue, or reduced engagement. Only a conversation can distinguish them.

Ask about workload, clarity, access, and support. Do not present the indicators as an accusation, and do not promise confidentiality you cannot keep.

## Separate sensitive cases

If the conversation raises a safety, harassment, or health concern, follow the company process and involve the right owner. Do not investigate informally or record unnecessary personal detail.

## Decide and record

For each case, record the observed signal, the conversation date, the agreed action, and the review date. Actions might include a scope correction, an access fix, a schedule change, or no change with continued monitoring.

## Limitations

The method cannot predict attrition, and it can produce false alarms. It should be used to prompt support, not to build a ranking.
