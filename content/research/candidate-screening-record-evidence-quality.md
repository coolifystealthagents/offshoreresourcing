## Research question

What makes a candidate screening record decision-useful when screening coordination is handled by a Philippines based specialist? The question is about evidence quality, not about choosing a preferred candidate. A record should help an authorized hiring manager compare the same role requirements without confusing a coordinator's notes with an employment decision.

The method is a review of structured selection, job-analysis, privacy, and internal-control guidance. The unit is a record field or evidence item linked to a defined requirement. It does not validate any individual assessment method for every jurisdiction.

## From impressions to evidence

O*NET provides a public structure for connecting occupations with tasks, knowledge, skills, and work activities. That approach supports a role-first screening record. A broad impression such as "good communicator" should be translated into an observable requirement, such as writing a concise response from a supplied case record, with a consistent scoring rule.

The record should distinguish source, observation, interpretation, and decision. Source may be a resume, work sample, or interview note. Observation describes what was actually provided. Interpretation explains how it relates to the requirement. Decision belongs to the authorized hiring owner. Keeping those fields separate reduces the risk that a coordinator's shorthand becomes an unsupported claim.

## Designing the Philippines support boundary

A Filipino screening coordinator can check whether required evidence is present, organize comparable records, and flag missing or conflicting information. The hiring manager should set the threshold, assess protected or sensitive matters lawfully, and make the advance or reject decision. The role should not infer health, family status, age, nationality, or other sensitive attributes from a record.

Use the same fictional work sample and scoring rubric for every applicant in the same role. Record the instruction, output, reviewer, and any permitted adjustment. If a candidate cannot be compared because the evidence is incomplete, mark the gap rather than filling it with a guess. A manager can then decide whether to request more information.

Privacy matters at the record level. Collect only what the screening purpose requires, restrict access, and define retention with the accountable owner. The Philippines National Privacy Commission and the ICO both emphasize purpose, proportionality, and responsible handling, but the correct legal rule depends on the parties and jurisdictions involved.

## Limitations

Structured records do not remove bias or guarantee predictive validity. A work sample can be unfair if the instructions assume tools or experience unrelated to the role. Public occupational data is a reference, not a candidate score. Privacy guidance is not legal advice, and employment decisions require local review.

## Conclusion

Decision-useful screening records show the requirement, evidence, observation, uncertainty, and accountable decision owner. Offshore Resourcing can place coordination in a Philippines based role when the boundary is explicit and comparable evidence is preserved. The record improves the manager's decision. It does not transfer selection authority to the coordinator.
