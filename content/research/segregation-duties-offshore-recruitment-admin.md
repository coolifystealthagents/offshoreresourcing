## Research question

Which recruitment-administration duties should remain separate when a Philippines based specialist handles candidate records? The question concerns control design, not trust in a person or a location. Recruitment data can influence access, employment decisions, and communications, so the role needs an accountable owner and a defensible audit trail.

The method is a review of NIST, government internal-control, and privacy guidance applied to common recruitment records. It distinguishes preparation, authorization, release, and review. It does not provide legal advice or a complete compliance program.

## The control logic

NIST defines separation of duties as dividing responsibilities so one person does not control a conflicting sequence. The U.S. Government Accountability Office similarly treats control activities and responsibilities as part of a functioning internal-control system. The practical point is simple: the person who enters or prepares a record should not automatically approve the decision that record supports.

For offshore recruitment administration, four duties often need review: creating a candidate record, changing a status, approving a decision, and exporting or disclosing information. A client may combine some duties in a small team, but the combination should be deliberate and documented. The role brief should state which action is allowed, which requires approval, and what event is logged.

## Applying it to a Philippines role

A Filipino coordinator can often collect approved information, check required fields, schedule an interview from agreed availability, and flag a missing consent or document. The hiring manager should decide whether to reject, advance, waive, or change a requirement. An authorized owner should approve external disclosure and access changes.

Least privilege is a companion principle. The specialist should see the systems and fields needed for the assigned task, not every record available to the organization. A review should check the account, role permissions, audit events, download controls, and the path for removing access when the assignment changes.

Segregation is not achieved by a policy sentence alone. Test it with a fictional candidate record and trace who can create, edit, approve, and review. If the system cannot enforce the boundary, use a compensating review and record the residual risk. The client remains responsible for the lawful basis, retention, and employment decision.

## Limitations

NIST and GAO guidance is general and does not replace Philippine or client-country privacy, employment, or sector requirements. A small organization may not have enough people for perfect separation. A second-person review reduces some risks but does not make an unauthorized process lawful or safe.

## Conclusion

Recruitment administration is easier to staff offshore when duties are separated by decision and consequence rather than by title. A Philippines based coordinator can prepare and route records within a clear boundary. Approval, access changes, and sensitive disclosure should remain with named owners, supported by least privilege and an auditable review.
