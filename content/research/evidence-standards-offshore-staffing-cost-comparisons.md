Cost comparisons in offshore staffing often start and end with an hourly rate. That number can be useful, but it rarely reflects the full cost of making a role productive, covering absences, managing quality, and ending the arrangement cleanly. A defensible comparison uses the same work scope, the same time period, and the same cost categories for every option. This protocol defines those categories and the evidence needed to support each one. Compare [offshore staffing services](/services) with the [research library](/research) when you translate this method into a review plan.

The method is designed for a buyer comparing a dedicated staff arrangement, a managed service, and an internal hire. It does not assume that one model is cheaper. The result should be a range with visible assumptions, not a single number that hides the differences between the options. The buyer can then decide which differences matter for the role and the risk.

## Research question and decision supported

The question is what each staffing option costs over a defined period for the same scope of work, including the costs that are easy to omit. The decision supported is which option to pursue, which costs to negotiate, and which assumptions need verification before signing. A second decision is whether a lower headline rate is offset by higher management, turnover, or setup cost. State the currency, the tax basis, and the exchange rate date before the first total is calculated.

The comparison should separate one-time costs from recurring costs and from contingent costs. One-time costs include recruiting, equipment, and setup. Recurring costs include wages, fees, software, and management time. Contingent costs include replacement, overtime, travel, and exit. A comparison that mixes them into one monthly figure can hide a large one-time cost or a risk that appears later.

## Population and unit of analysis

Define the population as the staffing options being compared for the same role and scope. Each option should be described with the same role brief, the same service window, the same deliverables, and the same quality standard. If an option cannot meet the scope, record the gap rather than adjusting the scope silently. The comparison is only meaningful when the work is comparable.

The unit is one option-year, meaning one staffing option over a twelve-month period. Use the same period for every option and the same volume assumptions. If the role ramps over time, break the year into phases. Do not compare a fully ramped internal hire with a new offshore hire in month one, because the comparison will confuse cost with timing.

## Definitions and measures

Define total cost as the sum of one-time, recurring, and contingent costs over the period, with each category shown separately. Define management cost as the estimated hours the buyer's team spends on supervision, review, scheduling, and exception handling, valued at a stated internal rate. Define replacement cost as the cost of recruiting and onboarding a replacement, weighted by the expected turnover assumption. Keep the categories visible in the final table so a reader can remove a disputed cost and see the effect.

Measure the cost per accepted output, not only the cost per hour. An option with a higher hourly rate may produce more accepted work per hour, which changes the comparison. Report the assumptions behind the output estimate. If the output cannot be measured, use a range and state what evidence would narrow it.

## Evidence and data dictionary

The data dictionary should include the option, the scope, the period, the volume, the wage or fee, the employer costs, the software costs, the equipment costs, the management hours, the replacement assumption, the exit cost, and the source for each number. Add a field for currency and exchange rate if the options are priced in different currencies. Add a field for the date the quote was received. Label every estimate with its source and the date it was collected so a later reviewer can refresh it.

Evidence should be a quote, a contract term, a published rate, or a documented internal estimate. A verbal estimate should be labeled as an assumption and assigned an owner to verify. Do not present a vendor's marketing claim as a measured cost. If a number cannot be verified, keep it in the range with a clear note.

## Sampling and collection

Collect quotes from more than one provider where possible, using the same role brief and the same questions. Ask each provider to separate the fee from the employer costs, the equipment, the software, and the replacement terms. Ask what is excluded. A quote that does not state its exclusions is not comparable.

Collect internal management estimates from the people who actually supervise the work. Ask them to log their time for two weeks rather than estimate from memory. Use the same internal rate for every option. If the management burden differs by model, record the difference and explain why.

## Reviewer independence and agreement

Have a finance reviewer and an operations reviewer examine the same comparison independently. Finance checks the arithmetic and the cost categories, while operations checks the scope and the management assumptions. Compare their conclusions before the final decision. A disagreement often points to an assumption that was not written down.

Agreement on the total does not prove that the comparison is fair. Two reviewers can agree on a number while using the same optimistic turnover assumption. Check the sensitivity of the result to the largest assumptions. The review should also ask whether the options are truly comparable or whether one has a hidden scope advantage.

## What the evidence can support

The evidence can support a negotiation position, a budget range, and a decision about which cost categories to verify before signing. It can show whether a lower fee is offset by management time or replacement risk. It can also show which assumptions have the largest effect on the total. These are practical inputs to a buying decision.

The evidence cannot predict the future cost of turnover or the actual quality of a provider. It cannot prove that the lowest-cost option is the best value. The comparison should be paired with a quality and continuity review. A cheap option that cannot cover the work is not cheap in operating terms.

## Limitations and error

Quotes are often incomplete. A provider may exclude equipment, software, overtime, or replacement, and the buyer may not notice until later. Management time is frequently underestimated because it is spread across many people. Turnover assumptions are uncertain and can dominate the result. Report each limitation beside the number it affects.

Currency and tax treatment can also distort the comparison. Use the same currency and the same tax basis for every option, and record the exchange rate date. If the contract is denominated in a different currency, show the sensitivity to a rate change. A comparison that ignores currency risk is incomplete for a cross-border arrangement.

## Sensitivity and alternatives

Rerun the comparison with a higher turnover assumption, a longer ramp period, and a lower management estimate. If the ranking changes, report the result as sensitive. Rerun with the one-time costs spread over two years instead of one. Rerun with a different volume assumption to see how the cost per output changes.

Consider alternatives before choosing a model. A smaller scope, a different shift, a shared role, or a phased start may change the economics more than a rate negotiation. Name the alternative and the trade-off. The goal is not the lowest number but the most defensible total cost for the work the buyer actually needs.

## Privacy, access, and retention

Keep vendor quotes and internal cost estimates in a restricted location. Use role labels rather than employee names in the working file. Do not include personal compensation details that are not needed for the comparison. If the analysis is shared, aggregate the sensitive figures.

Set a retention period for the quotes and the working file. Keep the final comparison and the assumptions for the period required by the procurement policy. Log access to the file. Cost data is commercially sensitive and should be handled with the same care as other confidential records.

## Reporting template

Report the options, the scope, the period, the volume, and the cost categories. Present the one-time, recurring, and contingent costs separately, with the source and the date for each number. Show the cost per accepted output and the sensitivity to the largest assumptions. Use the same table structure for every option so a missing category is obvious rather than hidden in prose.

Close with the recommended option, the assumptions to verify, the negotiation points, and the review date. If the evidence is incomplete, say so. A cost comparison is only useful when the reader can see what is included, what is excluded, and how much the answer depends on the assumptions. Name the person who will verify each open assumption before the contract is signed.
