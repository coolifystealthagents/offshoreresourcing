## Research question

Published September 2, 2026. Scope drift becomes measurable when teams record requests that fall outside the approved role instead of treating each request as an isolated favor.

## Findings

A weekly boundary review can compare new tasks, systems used, decisions requested, and manager approvals against the current role brief.

The practical control is a change record: proposed work, business reason, access, risk, training, reviewer capacity, decision owner, pilot period, and disposition.

## Practical takeaways

- Count out-of-scope requests by source and workflow.
- Separate preparatory tasks from decisions that require accountable authority.
- Pilot approved expansions before making them permanent.

## Control table

| Signal | Evidence | Accountable response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New task requested | Role brief and intake log | Role owner reviews fit |
| New permission needed | Access register | System owner approves least privilege |
| Decision made by contributor | Decision record and sample | Manager corrects authority boundary |
| Repeated exception | Exception log | Process owner repairs guidance |

## Implementation routine

Start with one current workflow and a short review period. Record the baseline, apply one agreed control, and compare the same measures at the next checkpoint. Contributors can assemble evidence and flag exceptions; managers retain decisions about access, staffing, policy, budget, employment, and customer commitments.
