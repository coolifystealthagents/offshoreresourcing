## Key takeaways

- Choose a model after defining the work, controls, hours, and decision owner.
- Direct hiring offers control but requires internal recruiting and employment operations.
- Managed staffing can reduce setup work, but provider terms and management quality matter.

## Comparison

| Model | Strong fit | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct hire | Mature internal people operations | More setup and employment ownership |
| Managed staffing | Teams wanting recruiting and delivery support | Provider margin and contract dependency |
| Specialist agency | Defined project or scarce skill | Less continuity for recurring operations |
| Freelancer | Small independent task | Capacity and replacement risk |
| Automation | Stable rules and digital inputs | Exceptions still need an owner |

Review [workforce planning support](/services/workforce-planning-support) and [talent sourcing support](/services/talent-sourcing-support) before comparing proposals.

## When Offshore Resourcing fits

Offshore Resourcing is a fit when a team wants a documented role, recruiting support, clear manager boundaries, and an operating scorecard. It is not the right answer when the work is undefined or the buyer expects a provider to own regulated decisions without oversight.

## A practical next step

Write five recurring tasks, required hours, systems, examples, and approval limits. Use the same brief with every option so the comparison remains fair.
